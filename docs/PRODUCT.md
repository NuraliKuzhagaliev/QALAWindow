# Product and business hypothesis

Residents, students, visitors and small groups often have a short gap between commitments. QALA turns a location, a usable time budget and explicit needs into one to three nearby actions with an inspectable walking calculation. It prioritizes relevant choices, context, short explanations, simple testing, useful additions, privacy and completeness in the official rubric's order. No numeric official weights have been supplied.

Flagship flows: an Expo-area 45-60 minute return loop, a Nurzhol-area group with mixed interests, and an independent judge changing the location, time or simulated closure. Coverage is deliberately bounded. Zero results are valid when evidence cannot establish a strict requirement.

The resident demo is free to use. Possible future buyers are hotel or university visitor desks and city tourism services. These are hypotheses: there are no claimed partners, revenue, adoption figures or paid pilots. Next validation: five to eight observed usability sessions and two buyer conversations, only with explicit authorization for outreach. Future sponsorship must be disclosed and cannot override hard constraints.

The distinguishing product hypothesis is an inspectable short city outing with a return calculation, preserved uncertainty and an understandable alternative. Conversational maps, group itineraries and offline travel tools already exist. No global novelty or winning claim is made.

Roadmap: verify venue entrance points and schedules, improve cultural-place coverage, review Kazakh with a native speaker, evaluate physical routes, and run a limited visitor-desk pilot. Events are deferred until a licensed, current feed exists. Voice, AR, bookings and payments remain out of scope.
